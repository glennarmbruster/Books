# Bridge | 4 | 45–75 min | Trick-taking | pairs | 0
Win tricks with a partner after bidding how many you can take. This four-deal home match uses contract scoring.
## Set the table
Remove the jokers. Sit opposite your partner. Call the seats North, East, South and West in clockwise order. South deals the first hand, one card at a time clockwise, until everyone has 13. Sort your hand by suit. Aces are high, followed by K Q J 10 down to 2. Keep your cards private during the auction.
## What is a trick?
Each person plays one card into the middle. Those four cards make one trick. The first card sets the suit everyone must follow if able. A player without that suit may play any card. The highest card in the led suit wins unless a trump is played, in which case the highest trump wins. The winner leads the next trick. No-trump means no suit can trump another.
## Bid for tricks
The dealer speaks first, then turns go clockwise. Pass, or bid a number from 1 to 7 and a suit or no-trump (NT). Add six to the number to find the promised tricks. For example, 2♥ promises eight tricks with hearts as trumps. At a single level the order from lowest to highest is ♣, ♦, ♥, ♠, NT. Every new bid must outrank the previous bid. After 1♠ you can bid 1NT or any bid at level two or higher.
You can pass now and bid later. You may double the opponents’ last bid if only passes have followed it. Its side may then redouble if only passes have followed the double. A fresh bid cancels the double or redouble. Four opening passes score zero. Otherwise three consecutive passes after a bid, double or redouble end the auction.
## Make your first bidding decision
For a starter estimate count A=4, K=3, Q=2, J=1 in your hand. With about 13 points, consider opening your longest suit at level one. With two five-card suits, the higher-ranking one leaves room to bid the other later. An opening 1NT commonly shows 15–17 points with a balanced hand (no shortage: 4–3–3–3, 4–4–3–2 or 5–3–3–2 cards in the four suits). These are partnership agreements. You can play a first hand with just natural bids and passes.
When partner opens one of a major suit (♥ or ♠), three or more cards in that suit usually give you a fit if partner has at least five. Roughly 25–26 points between you can be enough for game. Holding a weak hand without support, pass. With a fit and about 6–9 points, a simple raise from 1♠ to 2♠ invites partner to decide. Game contracts to recognize are 3NT, 4♥, 4♠, 5♣ and 5♦.
## Open the play
The final bid is the contract. On the winning side, whoever FIRST bid that final suit or NT is declarer. Declarer’s left-hand opponent makes the opening lead. Only then does declarer’s partner, called dummy, lay their 13 cards face up, grouped by suit with trumps on dummy’s right. Declarer chooses dummy’s cards on dummy’s turns. The two defenders each play their own hand. Keep one tally per trick, regardless of its cards’ values.
## Score a made contract
For this home match play four fresh deals. Each deal stands on its own. Deal 1 neither pair is vulnerable, deal 2 North–South is vulnerable, deal 3 East–West is vulnerable, deal 4 both are vulnerable. Vulnerability changes bonuses and penalties. Move the deal one seat clockwise each time, including after four passes. Highest total after four deals wins; equal totals tie.
If declarer meets the promise, only declarer’s pair scores. For each BID trick above six count 20 in ♣/♦ or 30 in ♥/♠. At NT count 40 for the first bid trick and 30 for each additional bid trick. Multiply this contract amount by 2 if doubled or 4 if redoubled. If it reaches 100, add a game bonus of 300 nonvulnerable or 500 vulnerable. Otherwise add 50 for a partscore. Unbid extra tricks cannot turn a partscore into a game.
Count each extra trick at 20 in a minor (♣/♦) or 30 in a major (♥/♠) or NT. If doubled, each extra trick is 100 nonvulnerable or 200 vulnerable; redoubling doubles those extra-trick values. Add 50 for making a doubled contract or 100 for a redoubled one. Bidding and making level six adds 500 nonvulnerable or 750 vulnerable; level seven adds 1,000 or 1,500 respectively. Add these slam bonuses to the game bonus.
## Score a defeated contract
Only defenders score. Each undoubled trick short costs 50 nonvulnerable or 100 vulnerable. Doubled and nonvulnerable: 100 for the first, 200 each for the second and third, 300 for each further trick. Doubled and vulnerable: 200 for the first and 300 for each additional trick. Redoubled penalties are twice the doubled penalties. At 4♠ taking nine tricks you are down one. If undoubled and vulnerable the defenders record 100.
## If your table plays rubber bridge
Use a score sheet with two team columns and a horizontal line. Both sides start nonvulnerable. Deal and play as above. After a made contract write only the bid trick value (including any multiplier) BELOW the line. Overtricks, double/redouble bonuses, slam bonuses and defenders’ penalties go ABOVE. Use the same trick and penalty values as above. Do not award the four-deal format’s partscore or game bonuses.
When one side accumulates 100 below the line, that side wins a game and becomes vulnerable. Draw a new line under both columns; both sides start the next game at zero below it. Earlier scores still count toward the rubber total. The first side to win two games ends the rubber. Add 700 above the line if opponents won no game or 500 if they won one. Sum everything above and below; higher total wins the rubber score. For this home version omit optional honors bonuses. The Keep score shortcut on this page is for the four-deal match; use ruled paper for a rubber.
## Try the practice deal
Lay out the four hands in the practice table below. South opens 1♠, West passes, North raises to 4♠, then East, South and West pass. South first named spades, so South declares, West leads and North is dummy. Your target is ten tricks. Follow the whole deal or stop after any trick and try choosing the next lead yourselves.

# Rummy | 2–6 | 20–40 min | Draw and discard | individual | 100
Empty your hand by arranging cards into sets and runs. This is basic straight rummy with aces low.
## Deal
Use 52 cards without jokers. Deal ten each for two players, seven each for three or four, or six each for five or six. Stack the rest face down and turn its top card face up next to it. These are the stock and the discard pile. The player left of the dealer starts. Play clockwise.
## Build a meld
A set is three or four cards of one rank in different suits. A run is three or more consecutive ranks in one suit. A♥ 2♥ 3♥ works; Q♥ K♥ A♥ does not. Each card belongs to only one meld. Display a new meld face up so everyone can see it.
## Take a turn
Draw exactly one card from the stock or the top of the discard pile. You may lay down one new meld and add any number of cards to exposed melds, including other players’ melds. Keep their ranks and suits intact. You may keep all your melds in your hand instead. Discard one card face up unless you just melded your last card. A card picked up from the discard pile cannot go straight back that turn.
For example, you hold 5♥ 6♥ 9♣ Q♠. Draw 7♥, put the three-heart run down and discard Q♠. You keep 9♣. On a later turn you could add 9♣ to a set of three nines. If the stock runs out, leave the top discard exposed and turn the remainder over as a fresh stock. If no draw is possible, redeal without scoring.
## End and score
The first player with no cards wins the deal. The winner scores the value of the cards everyone else still holds: ace 1, numbered cards face value, J/Q/K 10 each. An opponent holding K♦, 8♣ and A♠ gives you 19. Rotate dealer. First to 100 wins. Use single scoring even if someone melds their whole hand at once in this version.

# Gin Rummy | 2 | 25–45 min | Draw and discard | individual | 100
Make secret melds and decide when your unmatched cards are cheap enough to knock.
## Deal
Deal ten each from 52. Turn one card up beside the stock. Nondealer may take it or pass. If passed, dealer gets the same choice. If both pass, nondealer draws from stock. Whoever draws discards one, then turns alternate.
## Draw, group, discard
Each turn draw the stock’s top card or top discard and return one card to the discard pile. You cannot immediately return the discard you picked up. Keep melds hidden: three or four of a rank, or three or more consecutive cards of one suit, ace low. No card counts twice. Unmatched cards are deadwood: ace 1, faces 10, otherwise face value.
## Knock or go gin
After drawing, discard to leave ten cards. If deadwood totals 10 or less you may knock and lay out your hand. Opponent displays melds, then may add deadwood to your melds. A knocker with no deadwood declares gin and blocks these layoffs. If stock reaches two cards with nobody knocking, cancel the deal.
## Score
If knocker has less deadwood, score the difference. Knock on 6 against 14 after layoffs to score 8. If opponent ties or beats an ordinary knock, opponent gets 25 plus the difference. Gin scores 25 plus all opponent deadwood. Alternate dealers and play to 100, omitting end-of-match bonuses.

# Whist | 4 | 20–35 min | Trick-taking | pairs | 5
A partnership game for learning tricks before trying Bridge.
## Deal and trump
Partners sit opposite. Deal all 52 one at a time, clockwise. Turn the dealer’s last card face up to name trumps. It belongs to the dealer, who picks it up after the opening lead. Ace high.
## Play
Left of dealer leads any card. In clockwise order each player follows suit if possible; otherwise play any card. The highest trump wins; if nobody trumps, highest in the led suit wins. Winner leads next. Partners keep their tricks together.
## Score
After 13 tricks score one for each trick your partnership took beyond six. A 9–4 split gives the winning side 3. Rotate dealer. First pair to 5 wins.

# Hearts | 4 | 30–50 min | Trick-taking | low | 100
Avoid capturing hearts and especially the queen of spades.
## Deal and pass
Deal 13 each. Choose three cards before receiving replacements. Pass left on deal one, right on two, across on three, and keep all cards on four. Repeat this cycle. Rotate dealer each deal.
## Play
Holder of 2♣ leads it. Follow the suit led when able. If void, discard anything except a heart or Q♠ on trick one, unless only penalty cards remain. Highest card of the led suit wins and leads next; aces high, no trump. You cannot lead a heart until one has been discarded off-suit, unless your hand contains only hearts.
## Score
Each heart taken costs 1, Q♠ costs 13. If one player takes all 26, give the other three 26 apiece and that player zero. When anyone reaches 100 or more, lowest total wins. If lowest is tied, deal again until the tie breaks.

# Spades | 4 | 30–60 min | Trick-taking | pairs | 500
Predict how many tricks your partnership can take. Spades are always trump.
## Deal and bid
Partners sit opposite. Deal 13 each from 52. Starting left of dealer each player names their expected trick count. Add partners’ bids to get the contract. A bid of zero is nil, a separate promise to take no trick. For a first game omit blind bids.
## Play
Left of dealer leads a non-spade. Go clockwise, following suit if possible. If void, play anything. Highest spade wins; if there is none, highest of led suit wins. Ace high. Winner leads. Once someone trumps an off-suit trick, spades are broken and may be led; you can also lead spades if that is all you hold.
## Score
Make your combined bid for ten times the bid plus one per extra trick (a bag). Fall short and lose ten times the bid. With a bid of 7 and 9 tricks score 72 and record two bags. Every tenth accumulated bag costs 100 and uses up ten bags. Keep a separate bag tally. Successful nil adds 100; failed nil costs 100. Here a failed nil player’s tricks count toward the team bid. Play to 500; higher total wins, dealing again on a tie.

# Euchre | 4 | 20–35 min | Trick-taking | pairs | 10
Your partnership needs three of five tricks. The two most powerful cards are jacks.
## Deal and choose trump
Remove everything below 9, leaving 24. Partners face each other. Deal five each in packets of two and three; turn the next card up. From dealer’s left each may pass or order up its suit. If ordered up, dealer takes it and discards one face down. If all pass, turn it down. In a second round anyone may name another suit. Four more passes end the deal; rotate dealer and redeal.
## Know the bowers
Highest trump is its jack, then the other jack of the same color, then trump A K Q 10 9. That second jack belongs entirely to trump. With hearts trump, J♦ follows hearts and cannot follow diamonds. Other suits rank A K Q J 10 9, with the transferred jack removed.
## Play and score
The caller may say “alone” before the lead; their partner sits out. First active player left of dealer leads. Follow suit if able; highest trump or highest of led suit wins and leads. Callers taking three or four tricks score 1, all five score 2 (4 alone). Defenders taking at least three score 2. Rotate dealer. First to 10 wins.

# Oh Hell | 3–6 | 25–45 min | Trick-taking | individual | 0
Hit your exact bid, even if it means losing a trick on purpose.
## Deal
Use 52, ace high. For this short match deal one card each on hand one, two on hand two, up through seven and back down to one. Rotate dealer. Turn the next card up for trump.
## Bid and play
Starting left of dealer everyone predicts their own number of tricks, including zero. Dealer bids last and may not make the total bids equal the tricks available. Player left leads. Follow suit if possible, otherwise play any card. Highest trump wins, or highest card of the led suit if nobody trumps. Winner leads next.
## Score
Record one point per trick won and a ten-point bonus for hitting your bid exactly. Bid two and win two for 12; bid two and win three for 3. After the last one-card deal, high total wins; share a tie.
# German Whist | 2 | 15–25 min | Trick-taking | individual | 0
Build a strong hand in the first half. Only tricks in the second half count.
## Deal
Deal 13 each. Lay the other 26 face down and turn the top card up. Its suit is trump throughout. Nondealer leads. Aces high.
## First thirteen tricks
Lead one card; opponent must follow suit if able. If void, any card is legal. The trump wins against a nontrump; otherwise highest card of the led suit wins. Winner takes the exposed stock card, loser takes the next unseen card. Expose the next top card. Winner leads the next trick. Set played cards aside.
## Last thirteen tricks
With stock gone, play your remaining 13 cards under the same trick rules, with no replacement draws. Each trick won now is one point. Whoever takes at least seven wins. For a match swap dealers and compare total second-half tricks over two deals.

# Crazy Eights | 2–5 | 15–30 min | Shedding | individual | 100
Be first to empty your hand by matching the discard.
## Deal
Use 52, deal five each and turn one up beside the stock. If it is an eight, bury it and turn another. Start left of dealer; play clockwise.
## Play
Match either rank or suit of the top discard, or play an eight on any card and announce the suit the next player must match. On 9♦ you could play 9♣, 2♦ or any eight. If unable to play, draw until you can play or stock runs out. If empty, shuffle all but the top discard into a new stock. If no draw or play is possible, pass. A full circuit of passes ends the hand unscored.
## Score
An empty hand wins. Collect the points still held by opponents: eights 50, faces 10, aces 1, others face value. First to 100 wins.

# Go Fish | 2–6 | 10–20 min | Collecting | individual | 0
Collect books of all four cards of a rank.
## Deal
With two or three players deal seven each; otherwise deal five. Put the stock face down. Left of dealer begins.
## Ask
Name one opponent and a rank you already hold. They must give you every card of that rank. If they have any, ask again, possibly for a different rank or person. Otherwise draw one from stock. Show it and ask again if it matches what you requested; otherwise your turn ends. If stock is empty, a failed request ends your turn. Turns pass clockwise.
## Finish
Immediately put any four of a kind face up. If your hand is empty, draw one when stock permits, otherwise sit out until the end. Game ends when all 13 books are down. Most books wins; tied players share the win.

# Old Maid | 2–7 | 10–15 min | Matching | none | 0
Get rid of pairs and dodge the last unpaired queen.
## Deal
Take one queen out of a 52-card pack unseen and set it aside. Deal all 51 remaining cards around. Uneven hands are fine. Everyone lays down pairs of matching rank; a group of three leaves one in hand.
## Play
Left of dealer draws one unseen card from the player on their right. Make and discard a new pair if possible. Next player does the same, working clockwise. Fan your cards backs outward and shuffle their order. Skip players whose hands are empty both when drawing and offering cards.
## Finish
When all pairs are gone, exactly one queen remains. Its holder loses; everyone else has escaped.

# War | 2 | 10–40 min | Comparing | none | 0
Flip cards together and settle tied ranks with a war.
## Deal and battle
Split 52 into two face-down piles of 26. Each flips their top card. Higher rank (ace high) captures both into the bottom of the winner’s pile face down. Suits do not matter.
## Break a tie
Each places three cards face down and the next one face up. High face-up rank takes the whole pot. Repeat for another tie. If short of four, use your last remaining card face up. If you have no card to contest a war, you lose; if neither has a card left, draw.
## Finish
Owning the whole deck wins. For a time limit, finish the current battle when the timer rings and count cards; larger pile wins, equal piles tie.
# Concentration | 1–6 | 10–20 min | Memory | individual | 0
Find pairs by remembering cards everyone has seen.
## Lay out
For a short game use all four suits from ace through six (24 cards). Shuffle and lay face down in four rows of six. Pick a starting player and go clockwise.
## Turn two
Expose any two different cards and let everyone look. Same rank is a pair regardless of suit. Take the pair and go again. Otherwise turn both back in exactly the same spots and pass the turn.
## Finish
Most pairs after the grid empties wins; tie is shared. Solo, count your turns and try to clear the grid in fewer turns next time.
# Spoons | 3–8 | 10–20 min | Passing | none | 0
Grab a spoon when four cards match, or when someone else grabs first.
## Set up
Place one fewer spoon than players in reach of everyone. Deal four cards each from 52. Dealer takes the stock. Everyone keeps a hand of four between exchanges.
## Pass
Dealer draws one and passes one unwanted card face down to the left. Everyone else picks up one incoming card at a time and passes one left; only the last player discards into a waste pile. If stock empties, recycle the waste. Cards flow continuously.
## Finish
With four of a kind, quietly take a spoon. Once a spoon moves, anyone can take one. Player left without one loses the round and drops out. Remove a spoon, shuffle and deal again. Last survivor wins. If two remain they play for one spoon.

# Knockout Whist | 2–7 | 15–30 min | Trick-taking | none | 0
Take at least one trick to stay at the table as hands get smaller.
## Deal
Use 52, aces high. Start with seven cards each, or six for seven players if you prefer a shorter game. Turn the next stock card for trump. Player left of dealer leads. In subsequent hands deal one fewer card to each surviving player.
## Play
Everyone plays one in order, following suit if possible. Any card may be played when void. Highest trump wins, otherwise highest of led suit wins. Winner leads. Count each player’s tricks separately.
## Finish
In this no-extra-life version, anyone taking zero tricks is out. Rotate dealer amongst survivors; turn a fresh trump every hand. Last survivor wins. If several reach the one-card deal, that single trick decides it.
# GOPS | 2 | 10–15 min | Simultaneous bids | individual | 0
Bid cards for prizes and decide when to spend your ace-sized advantage.
## Set up
Give one player all spades and the other all hearts. Remove clubs. Shuffle diamonds face down as prizes. Aces count 1, J=11, Q=12, K=13 in every suit.
## Bid
Turn one diamond up. Both choose one bid from hand face down, then reveal together. Higher rank takes the diamond and its point value. Discard both bids forever. In this carryover version a tie leaves the prize in the middle and the next higher bid wins all waiting prizes.

# Six-Card Golf | 2–4 | 25–35 min | Draw and discard | low | 0
Finish nine deals with the lowest score in your two-by-three grid.
## Deal
Deal each player six face-down cards. Place them unseen in two rows of three; each player turns any two face up. Put one stock card face up beside the deck. Player left of dealer starts.
## Turn
Draw top stock or discard. Exchange it for any grid card, leaving the new card face up and discarding the replaced card without first peeking. If you drew stock you may instead discard it. Leave all other cards in place. If stock empties, shuffle buried discards into a new stock. Continue clockwise until one player has all six cards face up; everyone reveals immediately.
## Score
Two equal ranks in a single vertical column cancel to zero. Otherwise score each ace 1, each two −2, 3–10 face value, J/Q 10, K 0. Rotate dealer. Lowest nine-deal total wins, sharing a tie.

Collect four of one rank, then signal before anyone else notices.
## Deal
Choose one full rank per player from the deck. For five people use all four cards of five ranks. Shuffle and deal four each. Choose an agreed signal such as a finger touching the nose.

# Cheat | 3–7 | 10–25 min | Bluffing | none | 0
Empty your hand by announcing ranks truthfully or bluffing.
## Deal
Deal the whole 52-card deck. Uneven hands are fine. Left of dealer begins with aces. The required rank advances clockwise by turn: ace, two, three through king, then ace again.
## Play
Put one to four cards face down on the middle pile and announce how many of the required rank you played. They can be any ranks in your hand. Before the next play anybody may call “Cheat!” Reveal only the most recent cards. Any lie makes the player take the entire pile. If all matched, the challenger takes it.
## Finish
After a challenge, continue with the next player and the next rank. You win on playing your last cards once others decline to challenge or your final claim proves true. A caught final bluff returns the pile to your hand.
# Sevens | 3–8 | 15–25 min | Building sequences | none | 0
Empty your hand by extending the four suit rows on the table.
## Deal
Deal the entire pack, one at a time. The holder of 7♦ plays it first. Continue clockwise. Cards stay face up on the table.
## Play
On your turn play one seven to begin a new suit, or a card one rank above or below an exposed end in its own suit. A suit grows down from 7 to ace and up from 7 to king. With 6♥ and 7♥ down, 5♥ or 8♥ fits. You must play if able; otherwise pass.
## Finish
First empty hand wins immediately. To give equal hands value across a match, rotate dealers and play a fresh deal for each person at the table.
# Ninety-Nine | 2–6 | 10–20 min | Counting | none | 0
Keep a shared total at or below 99. Agree on these special cards before dealing.
## Deal
Give everyone three tokens and three cards. Keep remaining stock face down. Total starts at zero. Left of dealer starts, moving clockwise.
## Play
Play one card, say the new total, then draw to restore three cards. Aces add 1 or 11, 4 reverses direction without adding, 9 skips adding, 10 subtracts ten (floor of zero), kings set the total to 99. Other number cards add face value and J/Q add 10. A 4 sends play back to the previous player. In a two-player game either direction passes to the opponent. Recycle discards if needed.
## Finish
If every play would exceed 99, lose a token. Gather and reshuffle all cards, redeal, reset total to zero. A player who loses their last token drops out. Last player with tokens wins.
# Snap | 2–6 | 5–15 min | Quick recognition | none | 0
Spot the same rank on two different face-up piles.
## Deal
Deal the whole pack into roughly equal face-down stacks. Nobody looks at them. Left of dealer begins. On a turn flip your top card away from yourself onto your own face-up pile.
## Call
If the new card matches the top rank of another player’s face-up pile, first to call “Snap!” takes both face-up piles and puts them under their stock. Only the newly created match counts. Agree to leave cards in place and continue on simultaneous calls or false calls.
## Finish
When your stock empties, turn your face-up pile over to make a new stock without shuffling. If you have neither, drop out. Last player wins. For a short game, stop at a set time; count all your cards, most wins.

# Clock Patience | 1 | 5–10 min | Solitaire | none | 0
Reveal all 52 cards before the fourth king turns up.
## Lay out
Deal 13 face-down piles of four. Place twelve like clock hours and the thirteenth in the center. Ace belongs at one o’clock, jack at eleven, queen at twelve, king in the middle.
## Play
Turn the top card of the middle pile. Slip it face up underneath its matching hour pile (a king goes under the middle). Turn the top face-down card from the pile you just visited. Repeat. Keep uncovered cards face up beneath each pile.
## Finish
If the fourth king is the last hidden card you win. If it arrives earlier, the center has no face-down card to turn and the game ends.
# Pyramid | 1 | 10–15 min | Solitaire | none | 0
Clear a triangular layout by pairing exposed cards that add to thirteen.
## Lay out
Deal seven overlapping rows, from one card at the top to seven across the base, all face up. Each card covers parts of the two just above. Keep the other 24 face down as stock.
## Play
Only completely uncovered pyramid cards and the waste pile’s top are available. Remove K alone or two available cards totaling 13. Ace is 1, J 11, Q 12. A queen pairs with an ace; two cards of the same rank cannot sum to 13. Turn stock cards onto the waste one at a time. In this version there is one stock pass.
## Finish
Win once all 28 pyramid cards are cleared. Waste and stock may remain. If you exhaust stock and no legal removal remains, redeal for another try.
# Klondike | 1 | 10–20 min | Solitaire | none | 0
Build four suit piles from ace to king.
## Lay out
Deal seven columns of increasing lengths 1 through 7. Only the bottommost visible card of each column faces up; earlier cards face down. Keep the remaining 24 as stock. Leave four spaces above for the foundations.
## Play
Place an ace into its suit foundation and build upward in that suit. On columns build down one rank in alternating red and black. Move exposed ordered runs together. Turn a newly uncovered face-down card up. Only a king or a sequence starting with a king may fill an empty column. Turn stock one at a time onto a waste pile; only its top is available.
## Finish
When stock empties turn the waste face down without shuffling for another pass. This version allows unlimited passes. Win with all four kings on completed foundations; if no moves improve the position, start again.
# Accordion | 1 | 5–15 min | Solitaire | none | 0
Compress the deck into a single pile, moving only to the left.
## Deal
Turn cards face up in a row left to right one at a time. Leave room to close gaps; you can wrap into a second row while keeping one continuous order.
## Play
Move a whole pile onto its immediate left neighbor or onto the pile exactly three places to its left if their top cards share rank or suit. Close the gap immediately. You may choose among legal moves and may move before or after dealing another card.
## Finish
After all 52 are dealt, continue merging until blocked. One pile wins. Record how few piles remain if you want to beat your last attempt.
# Thirty-One | 2–7 | 15–25 min | Draw and discard | none | 0
Keep the highest value you can in ONE suit across your three cards. This version uses three lives.
## Deal
Give everyone three tokens and three cards. Turn one discard up beside the stock. Ace counts 11, faces 10 and numbers their printed value. Add only one suit: A♥ 9♥ K♣ scores 20. Three of a kind has no special value in this version.
## Draw or knock
Starting left of dealer, draw top stock or top discard and discard one. You cannot immediately throw back the discard you took. Instead of drawing you may knock and keep your hand. # Five-Card Draw | 2–6 | 15–30 min | Poker | individual | 0
Practice draw poker with table counters. Agree on fixed stakes of one counter per action and play a set number of hands.
## Ante and deal
Give everyone 40 counters. Each contributes one to the pot. Deal five private cards each. Left of dealer starts the first betting round. If no bet is open, check (stay for free) or bet one. With a bet open, fold, call by matching it, or raise by one. Allow one raise per round; everyone still in must match it or fold. All checks, or matched bets after all have acted, ends betting. Last player left takes the pot immediately.
## Draw and show
Starting left of dealer, remaining players discard zero to three face down and draw back to five. Hold discards aside. If stock runs short, shuffle earlier players’ discards for replacements, excluding cards discarded by the player currently drawing. Bet again from left of dealer, then show. Best five-card hand takes the pot. Split exact ties; odd counter goes to first tied seat clockwise from dealer. Rotate deal.
## Poker hand guide
Strongest first: straight flush (five consecutive of one suit), four of a rank, full house (three of one rank and two of another), flush (five same suit), straight (five consecutive), three of a kind, two pairs, one pair, high card. A royal flush is the ace-high straight flush. A can end A–2–3–4–5 or 10–J–Q–K–A; no wrapping. Example: 9♣ 9♦ 9♥ 4♣ 4♠ beats any flush.
## Break ties and finish
Compare the rank making the combination, then remaining cards highest first. For two pair compare higher pair, lower pair then kicker. For full houses compare threes first. Straights compare the top card (5 in an ace-low straight). Flush or high card compares all five in order. Suits are equal. Play one orbit of the dealer; most counters wins. Counters have no cash value.

If unable to play, draw stock cards until you can, then play. If stock runs out and you still cannot play, pass. The highest card in the suit led wins the trick, ace high. A matching rank of a different suit lets you play but cannot win. Winner leads the next trick. Set finished tricks aside.
## Finish
As soon as someone plays their last card they say “Boom!” and win, even before the rest of the trick is played. Shuffle and rotate dealer for another hand.
# Stealing Bundles | 2–4 | 10–15 min | Capturing | individual | 0
Match the rank on top of somebody’s captured pile to steal the whole thing.
## Deal
Deal four each and four face up in the middle. Rest are stock. Left of dealer begins. Keep one face-up capture bundle per player with its top rank readable.
## Turn
Play one card. Match a loose middle card by rank and take both, or match the top of an opponent’s bundle and take that whole bundle plus your played card. If you can do either, choose one. You may add a matching card to your own bundle instead. Put your played card on top. With no match, leave it loose in the middle.
## Finish
Once hands run out deal up to four more each evenly; at four players with a 52-card pack after the opening table there are exactly three four-card hands apiece. Continue until stock and hands empty. For three players too, 48 cards divide evenly; use the same procedure. Last capturer takes the remaining loose cards. Largest bundle by number of cards wins.

# Manille | 4 | 15–30 min | Trick-taking | pairs | 0
Tens outrank aces in this French partnership trick game.
## Deal
Use 7 through ace, 32 cards. Partners opposite, deal eight each. Dealer declares a trump suit after seeing their hand; for this version always choose one suit. Rank high to low 10, A, K, Q, J, 9, 8, 7. Player left leads; play clockwise.
## Follow
Follow suit if possible. If an opponent currently leads the trick, beat it when you legally can in the led suit. If unable to follow and opponents are winning, trump if possible and overtrump when able. With partner winning and no led suit, discard freely. Highest trump wins; otherwise highest of led suit wins and leads next.
## Score
Captured 10=5, A=4, K=3, Q=2, J=1. Each of the eight tricks adds one point, giving 68 total. A team over 34 records its surplus; 41 scores 7. Rotate dealer. Play four deals and compare total surplus points.
# Canasta | 4 | 60–90 min | Draw and discard | pairs | 5000 | 2
Form long sets with your partner. Use two packs with both jokers from each, 108 cards in all.
## Deal and values
Partners sit opposite. Deal 11 each, turn a discard. If it is a wild card or red three, turn another until an ordinary card is visible; leave those underneath to freeze the pile. Immediately display any red threes from hand and draw replacements. If a stock draw is a red three, display and replace it. Values: joker 50; ace or 2 worth 20; 8 through king 10; 4–7 and black threes 5.
## Meld
Turn clockwise, starting left. Draw one stock card or legally take the entire discard pile, meld if desired, then discard one. A meld is at least three of one rank, with at least two natural cards and no more than three wild cards (2s and jokers). One team meld per rank. Build to seven for a canasta. Seven natural cards with no wilds is a natural canasta. You may add to a completed canasta.
## Open and pick up
A team’s first melds on one turn must reach a minimum card value: 15 if team score is below zero, 50 at 0–1495, 90 at 1500–2995, 120 at 3000 or more. Count the visible discard if melded but not buried cards or bonuses. A wild discard freezes the pile until taken. Against an unopened team it is also frozen. To take a frozen pile you must have two natural cards in hand matching its top, meld those three first and meet any opening minimum. Otherwise a legal top may join your existing meld or form a new legal meld from hand. A top wild or black three blocks pickup. Then take everything underneath.
## End and score
Go out by emptying your hand, with or without a final discard, only after your team has a canasta or completes one that turn. Black threes can be melded only as a natural group of three or four while going out. This home version ends the deal when stock is empty and the next player declines or cannot take discards. Add all melded card values, subtract all remaining hand values. Add 500 per natural canasta, 300 per mixed, and 100 for going out. Red threes add 100 each (all four 800 instead), or subtract those amounts if your team never melded. Omit concealed-out bonuses here. Higher team at 5000 or more at deal end wins.

# Pinochle | 4 | 35–60 min | Trick-taking | pairs | 150 | 2
Bid, show combinations, then win counters. Make a 48-card pinochle deck from two copies of A,10,K,Q,J,9 in each suit.
## Deal and auction
Partners opposite, deal 12 each. Rank A,10,K,Q,J,9. Use this single-point home tariff. Left of dealer opens at 15 or passes. In clockwise order raise by at least one or pass; passed players stay out. Highest bidder names trump after the others pass. If all pass, dealer takes it for 15. No partner card exchange here.
## Count meld before play
Each player shows scoring combinations in their own hand and records the team sum. Trump A-10-K-Q-J run scores 15, trump K-Q marriage 4, other marriage 2, Q♠+J♦ pinochle 4, trump 9 scores 1. One ace in each suit scores 10, kings around 8, queens around 6, jacks around 4. A card may count in different kinds of meld but only once within each kind. The marriage inside a run gives no separate 4. For this first-game tariff duplicate complete combinations count twice; do not use tournament double-meld multipliers. Return all meld cards to their owners’ hands.
## Tricks
Bidder leads. Follow suit and beat the winning card if possible while following; if void, trump if able and beat an existing trump if possible. Otherwise discard. First of two identical winning cards wins the trick. Winner leads next. Captured aces, tens and kings score 1 each; last trick adds 1.
## Settle
A team must win a trick to count meld. If the bidding team’s meld plus trick points reaches its bid, add actual points; if short, subtract the bid and add nothing else. Defenders add their earned points. Rotate dealer. Compare totals after each deal; highest team at or above 150 wins, tied teams play on.
# Hand and Foot | 4 | 60–90 min | Draw and discard | pairs | 0 | 5
Each player works through two hands. This partnership table uses five decks, each with two jokers.
## Deal
Give each player two piles of 11. Pick up the first (hand); keep the second (foot) unseen. Partners sit opposite. Put stock in the center and turn an ordinary 4–ace to start the discard, burying other starters. Left of dealer begins. Jokers and twos are wild. For this home version all threes are discard-only blockers.
## Turn and meld
Draw two from stock, or pick up exactly seven discards if at least seven are present, top is a natural 4–ace, and you hold two natural cards of that rank. Immediately meld those two with the top. You may meld other cards and must discard one unless going directly into your foot or going out by melding. Each team builds one open book per rank (4 through ace), three or more to start. Keep more naturals than wilds and at most two wilds per book in this version. Seven cards close a book; no further additions. Open a fresh book of that rank later if desired.
## Foot and finish
If you meld your last hand card, pick up your foot and continue the same turn. If you discard your last hand card, pick up the foot on your next turn. To go out, both partners must have picked up their feet, and team needs two natural and two mixed completed books. Ask partner before going out and honor their answer. If stock has fewer than two cards, draw what remains, finish that turn and score.
## Score four deals
For successive deals opening meld minimums are 50, 90, 120, 150 in card values from that turn; the top pickup may count but buried discards cannot. A joker is 50, twos and aces 20, 8–K 10, 4–7 and black threes 5. Red threes left in hand or foot cost 100; they score nothing discarded. Add values of melded cards, subtract remaining hand AND foot cards, add 500 per natural book, 300 per mixed and 100 for going out. Highest total after four deals wins.

# Elevens | 1 | 5–10 min | Solitaire | none | 0
Pair numbers to eleven and clear face cards in trios.
## Deal
Lay nine cards face up in a three-by-three grid. Keep the remaining deck face down. Suits are irrelevant; ace counts one.
## Play
Remove any two number cards totaling eleven (ace with 10, 2 with 9, 3 with 8, 4 with 7 or 5 with 6). Alternatively remove a jack, queen and king together. Never count face cards as tens. Immediately refill emptied spots from stock while possible.
## Finish
After stock is exhausted continue removing groups without refilling. Empty both stock and layout to win. If no legal group exists while cards remain, the deal is lost.
# Blackjack | 2–7 | 10–20 min | Comparing | individual | 0
Practice twenty-one with points. One person deals; each other hand plays directly against the dealer.
## Deal and values
Deal each player two face up; dealer takes one face up and one face down. Numbers count face value, faces count ten, each ace counts 1 or 11, whichever gives the highest total of 21 or less. A two-card ace plus ten-value card is blackjack. Dealer checks their hidden card for blackjack when showing ace or ten-value. If dealer has it, settle immediately.
## Hit or stand
Starting left of dealer, a non-busted player may take another card (hit) or keep the hand (stand). More than 21 busts immediately. A blackjack stands. After players finish, dealer reveals and hits on 16 or less, stands on every 17 or more, including soft 17. For this practice game omit insurance, splits and doubling. Shuffle the whole pack between deals; if it runs out mid-deal, void and redeal.
## Score and finish
For each opponent independently give 1 point to player or dealer for winning their comparison, 0 on a push. A player bust loses even if dealer later busts. Blackjack beats a non-blackjack 21; two blackjacks push. Otherwise higher unbusted total wins or surviving player beats a busted dealer. Rotate dealer each deal so everyone deals equally. Highest points after one full rotation wins.
